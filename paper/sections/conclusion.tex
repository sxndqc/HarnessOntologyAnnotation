\section{Conclusion}

Can LLMs build better annotators? On Chinese UMR, in a specific sense, yes: from a few gold documents an LLM builder writes the scheme's conventions into a harness, and the annotator gains \gainF{} F on documents it has not seen. It does not get better at the judgments that make semantic annotation hard; its rules accumulate, follow the latest document when annotators disagree, and stop paying off after the first few documents; and the builder must be strong. Its own suggestions say what comes next: code, context and consistent gold rather than more prose. A loop that tests edits before keeping them, admits code under tests, consolidates, and returns gold conflicts to people is the next step, and a sequential protocol with a paired seed is how to tell whether it works.

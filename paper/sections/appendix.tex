\section{The Judge}
\label{app:judge}

AnCast++ aligns each predicted node to at most one gold node. The judge then scores 25 checks in 11 groups over the aligned graphs; each check yields units with a verdict (correct, partial, wrong, missing, extra) and a credit, and reports F over its units. Three checks never fire on this corpus (granularity, the modal-strength attribute, which is moved to the document level, and Wikipedia links).

\section{Admission of Tools and Steps}
\label{app:review}

\paragraph{Machine checks.} A new step must load in the registry, attach after an existing step of the right chain, and keep its instruction prefix constant across calls. A new tool may import only \texttt{re}, \texttt{json}, \texttt{math}, \texttt{itertools}, \texttt{collections}, \texttt{functools}, \texttt{unicodedata}, \texttt{string} and \texttt{penman}; an AST check rejects \texttt{open}, \texttt{eval}, \texttt{exec}, \texttt{getattr} and access to underscored attributes, and requires exactly one public function with type annotations and a docstring. The proposal brings at least three tests, run in an isolated subprocess, all of which must pass. Nothing in a proposal may share eight characters with a corpus sentence.

\paragraph{Reviewer.} A separate call of the builder model receives the pipeline, the proposal, the machine-check results, the problems it addresses, all problem counts, the judge scores and the current skills. It approves only if all six criteria clearly hold and rejects when in doubt: (1) the problems are many, costly and will recur; (2) an edit to the skill that owns the aspect could not do as well; (3) one narrow responsibility that no skill has, in the right place, not fighting another skill; (4) worth its cost: a step is one more call per sentence and a new chance to break a correct graph, a tool must do what a model cannot do reliably (an exact lookup, a computation, a well-formedness check); (5) correct and safe, consistent with the other skills, with real tests; (6) no leakage.

\section{The Builder's Suggestions}
\label{app:suggestions}

Each round's plan may list changes the loop cannot apply. We coded all \nSuggestions{} (file \texttt{data/umr/suggestions\_coded.csv}) into five codes: \emph{gold} (\sugGold): a convention that differs between documents or annotators, or an error in gold, usually with a decision not to learn from it; \emph{rule code} (\sugRule): a deterministic change to a rule step, the output handler or a validator; \emph{context} (\sugContext): more input to a skill, such as earlier sentences, a running list of coreference chains, or the document's genre; \emph{evaluator} (\sugEval): a change to scoring, for instance normalizing equivalent quantity forms; \emph{other} (1). Examples, verbatim: ``Check the gold argument order of but-91 for concessives [\ldots] The prompts follow the majority'' (gold, round 1); ``Make final\_graph (or the output handler) normalise attribute values before choosing a graph'' (rule code, round 1); ``Give the document step a running list of coreference chains for the whole document so far'' (context, document 0006); ``Have the scorer report AnCast node-matching cascades separately from real concept errors'' (evaluator, document 0003). \gap{G13: a second coder and agreement.}

\paragraph{TimeML.} Test: the 20 TempEval-3 platinum documents with the official scorer \citep{uzzaman2013semeval}; training documents in a fixed order from TimeBank and AQUAINT (TBAQ-cleaned). The harness marks time expressions and events per sentence (\skill{mark}, with a normalization tool for values, then \skill{critique}), then writes TLINKs per document (\skill{link}, \skill{link\_critique}, with a link-checking tool). The 2013 systems are rescored from their released outputs with the same scorer, which reproduces their published numbers. Zero-shot: the seed's instructions and the whole document in one call; few-shot: the same with the ten training documents and their gold as examples.

\paragraph{Manifesto coding.} English sentences of electoral programs with one of eight domains of the Manifesto Project coding scheme (dataset \texttt{siacus/manifesto} on HuggingFace). The dataset has no document identifiers, so a ``document'' is a random batch of 120 sentences: ten training batches and one test set of 800 sentences. The harness is \skill{classify} with a codebook lookup tool, then \skill{critique}. Few-shot uses the 30 training sentences most similar to the input (TF-IDF).

\paragraph{Admission in these runs.} Both use machine checks only, without the reviewer, and also allow the builder to rewrite rule code, add or remove steps, and change the registry; a change that fails its checks is dropped.

\paragraph{Qwen3.5-9B.} Served with vLLM, thinking on, as both annotator and builder, on the procedural harness, sequentially over documents 0008--0035. Its anchors are often inexact (with strict matching, 1 of its first 15 edits applied), so its runs use fuzzy anchors (a unique line with similarity at least 0.85). Two independent runs of the seed estimate annotation noise.

\paragraph{DeepSeek.} The same model annotates and builds, on the procedural harness: two rounds each on documents 0001 and 0004, test on 0002, 0003 and 0005--0007; thinking was off in these runs.

\subsection{The learned harness is better on unseen documents}
\label{sec:main}

On the documents that also have the zero-shot prompt (0002--0007; Table~\ref{tab:prequential}), the systems form a ladder: \zeroMeanZ{} F for one call per sentence, \seedMeanZ{} for the seed harness, \firstMeanZ{} for the learned harness. Over the \nPaired{} documents with a seed control, the learned harness gains \gainF{} F (95\% CI \gainFLo--\gainFHi; Wilcoxon $p=$~\gainFWilcoxon) and improves \gainFPos{} of them; on the comprehensive score it gains \gainC{} (\gainCLo--\gainCHi) and improves all \gainCPos{} ($p=$~\gainCWilcoxon). Of \nSentPaired{} paired sentences, \sentBetter\% get higher Smatch and \sentWorse\% lower (mean +\sentGain, \sentGainLo--\sentGainHi). The one document that does not improve, 0008 (\gainMin), has ten sentences.

\paragraph{Training fit is not generalization.}
Three rounds on a document raise its own F by \trainFitGain{} points, to \afterMean{} on average and up to \afterMax, against \gainF{} on the next, unseen document (Figure~\ref{fig:curve}a). The leakage guard stops copying, not learning one document's particular conventions. Evaluated on its training documents, the same loop would look three times better.

\subsection{Where the gains come from}
\label{sec:where}

\paragraph{The generator, not the checker.}
Scoring the graph after each step (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:critique}) shows that the learned \skill{generate} alone is \genGain{} F better than the seed's on unseen documents, and \skill{critique} adds \critFirst{} on top (\critFirstMin{} to +\critFirstMax), less than the \critSeed{} it adds to the seed's draft; on the document it was trained on it adds \critAfter. The common-error lists learn to fix their own document; what transfers is in the generator's instructions. A variant with five specialized checks shows this more sharply: the checks add +\refineTrainMin{} to +\refineTrainMax{} on their training documents and \refineUnseen{} on unseen ones (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:refine}). At annotation time a checker has no gold, only what it was told, as self-correction studies would predict \citep{huang2024large,kamoi2024when}.

\paragraph{Convention, not competence.}
By judge check (Figure~\ref{fig:category}), the largest gains are on the scheme's conventions: pronoun person and number (+\catRefer{} points), the author's modal strength (+\catModal), non-core role labels (+\catPeriph), arguments of abstract rolesets such as \texttt{have-org-role-91} (+\catAbstract), non-core arguments of events (+\catPeriphEvent), entity concepts (+\catEntity). Checks that need a semantic judgment the model already makes as well as it can do not move: PropBank sense (+\catSense), aspect (+\catAspect), core arguments of events (+\catCoreEvent). Coreference (+\catCoref) gains little and polarity falls (\catPolarity, few units). The builder mostly teaches the annotator how the scheme writes things down, which is what a guideline is for.

\subsection{Tools are proposed rarely and admitted never}
\label{sec:tools}

In \nRounds{} rounds the builder proposed \nProposals{} tools and no steps; the reviewer rejected all three. \texttt{check\_umr\_graph} checked attribute values, entity types and role names; the reviewer kept the purely mechanical checks (colon-prefixed or misspelled closed-list values, parse failures, duplicate variables) and judged the rest to be missing conventions that belong in the instructions. \texttt{check\_umr\_form} flagged bare constants and aspect on discourse rolesets; the second part contradicted the builder's own earlier rule. \texttt{check\_doc\_relations} checked coreference on name nodes and double temporal parents, but one test contradicted a convention of \skill{document}. Each time a sound core was lost, because rejected proposals are not revised. Without the reviewer (the TimeML and manifesto runs), the builder changes tools or rules in most rounds \gap{G7: does admitting them help?}.

\subsection{More documents, more text, the same gain}
\label{sec:growth}

The gain does not grow with the number of documents behind it (Figure~\ref{fig:curve}b): +\gainFirstHalf{} on average for documents 2--6, +\gainSecondHalf{} for 7--10 (Spearman $\rho=$~\gainRho, $p=$~\gainRhoP). The harness grows from \sizeSeed{} to \sizeLast{}\,KB (\sizeGen{} in \skill{generate}, \sizeCrit{} in \skill{critique}, \sizeDoc{} in \skill{document}), \sizePerRound{}\,KB per round. Of \nEditsApplied{} applied edits, \nModify{} modify, \nAdd{} add and \nDelete{} delete (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:edits}): nothing rewards a shorter harness. Whether the plateau comes from what one document can teach, from interference among accumulated rules, or from length is open; held-out scores of $H_1$ to $H_{10}$ and a consolidation pass under a size budget would separate these \gap{G5, G6}.

\subsection{The builder must be capable}
\label{sec:models}

The gain holds for the procedural harness with the same models and for DeepSeek building and annotating without thinking (Table~\ref{tab:models}). With Qwen3.5-9B building and annotating over \qwenSmallN{} documents, the learned harness is worse than the seed (\qwenSmallGain{} F on average; better on \qwenSmallPos{} documents), and the decline deepens (+\qwenSmallFirst{} in the first half, \qwenSmallSecond{} in the second); two independent seed runs differ by \qwenSeedNoise{} F per document, so single documents are within noise, the trend is not. On agentic tasks, by contrast, a 9B model's harness updates were as useful as a frontier model's \citep{lin2026harness}. Whether builder or annotator matters more here is not yet separated \gap{G8}.

\subsection{Beyond UMR}
\label{sec:beyond}

The same loop builds a TimeML annotator for TempEval-3 \citep{uzzaman2013semeval} and a coder that assigns manifesto sentences one of the eight policy domains of the Manifesto Project scheme \citep{lehmann2026manifesto}, with the same protocol (Appendix~\ref{app:other}; finished baselines in Tables~\ref{tab:timeml} and~\ref{tab:manifesto}). On both test sets the seed harness is below the zero-shot prompt (TimeML event F1 74.5 vs.\ 80.7; manifesto accuracy 63.8 vs.\ 65.0), so the learned harness must first make up for its own seed. \gap{G10, G11: learned harness on both test sets; sequential gains.}

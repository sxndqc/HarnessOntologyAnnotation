## Summary of changes

1. **Amounts reached.** This was the most costly error. I rewrote the rule so the verb of reaching or falling is kept as the event and stays the root. Its theme is the measured noun as `:ARG0`, it gets `:aspect performance`, and the time words and adverbs attach to it. `have-quant-91` hangs on the measured noun with no aspect, no temporal parent and no modal parent. I also added that a sentence-initial connective linking only to the previous sentence is not a one-argument `but-91` root.
2. **Measure compounds.** The inner verb now gets no `:aspect` and no modal triple. It keeps only its `:overlap` with the governing verb.
3. **Formats and parts of publications.** When a format, layout or section noun is created or changed, that noun is the argument and the publication is its `:part-of`. In 从/由 A 变为/改为 B, A is `:ARG1` and B is `:ARG2`.
4. **Trailing clauses.** A trailing clause that says what the object holds or does goes under the object as `:ARGn-of` with `:aspect process`. It is not `:purpose`.
5. **Role nouns next to names.** A role or title noun before or after a person's name uses the role predicate, not `identity-91`. The critique reports gold uses `have-org-role-91` here, while the instruction's table gives the `-92` name. I followed the critique and assumed the Chinese data use the `-91` names (as Chinese AMR does). I could not verify this, and it applies only to Chinese documents.
6. **Abstract predicates inside noun phrases.** Role and appositive reifications that only modify a noun get no aspect, no temporal triple and no modal triple. The rule giving `identity-91` `:aspect state` now applies only when it is the clause predicate.
7. **Temporal links.** A date and the event in its clause are linked with `:overlap`, never `:contains`. A sentence's anchor is linked to an earlier sentence's event only when the text marks the sequence; otherwise a past event is `(document-creation-time :before e)`.
8. **Smaller points.** A decade is written as `:era` plus the year number only. I added a schematic example of a periodical's printing with a colour or format word.

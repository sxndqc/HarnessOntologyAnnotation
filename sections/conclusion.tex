\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Complex annotation can be bootstrapped from a guideline and a few examples. Without fine-tuning, an LLM that receives the guideline as a chain of steps, tools over the project's resources, and a loop that revises them against a handful of gold sentences annotates UMR better than the fine-tuned systems of its shared task on the same documents. The gains come from the harness: its decomposition into steps, its tools, and recursive self-improvement guided by a fine-grained judge, which plays the role that moderation plays in a human annotation project. Editing the guideline corrects notation; the steps and tools improve the annotation itself. In the loop, the builder model learned to edit the guideline, to write the tools the guideline presupposes, and to turn recurring decisions into code.

The loop learns the conventions of the gold it sees. It learned which TimeML events to link, learned to link less from a sparser corpus, and, given an explicit linking step, learned the gold's linking practice in one round. Senses and arguments improved where a tool supplied the frames; aspect, coreference and temporal relations, which no resource supports, moved little; and the builder's own suggestions point mostly to inconsistencies in the gold. This suggests a division of labor for annotation projects. The guideline becomes an executable object, tested and revised against data from the first annotated sentences on: the model maintains the instructions, tools and checks, and people decide the conventions and adjudicate the inconsistencies it surfaces. A new scheme or language then starts from a working annotator.

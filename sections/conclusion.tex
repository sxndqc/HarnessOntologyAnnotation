\section{Conclusion}
In the reported Chinese UMR trajectory, supervised instruction revision improves a modular annotator by \gainF{} sentence-graph F-score points over its seed on paired, previously untrained-on documents. Gains concentrate in convention-sensitive checks and are largely present at the generator stage. The same run exposes instruction growth, inconsistent rules and unsuccessful tool proposals. These findings describe one small, fixed-order study; they do not establish which design component causes the gains, a semantic-capability ceiling, or superiority to matched gold-budget alternatives. Held-out baselines, attribution and editing ablations, repeated trajectories, and builder--annotator swaps are the remaining tests of those claims.

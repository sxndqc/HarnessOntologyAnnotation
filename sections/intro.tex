\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Structured annotation follows a scheme of labels, relations and conventions: UMR graphs \citep{vangysel2021designing,bonn2024building}, TimeML \citep{pustejovsky2003timeml}, or political codebooks \citep{lehmann2026manifesto}. Even revising model drafts requires substantial human work \citep{sun2024chinese}. General-purpose LLMs may recognize semantic relations yet fail to follow the scheme. An annotation \emph{harness} supplies step instructions, tools, call chains and deterministic repairs; its construction typically requires repeated error analysis.

We ask whether an LLM can improve a manually initialized harness from a few gold documents. Guideline refinement revises instruction text against gold \citep{kim2026refining}; agent optimization can revise prompts and workflows \citep{zhang2026self,hu2025automated}. Structured annotation supplies aligned local discrepancies in addition to a scalar score, but identifying a useful repair target, avoiding instance-specific edits and evaluating transfer remain difficult.

\sys (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}) records step traces, assigns candidate edit targets, and applies typed, anchored instruction edits. Proposed tools and steps require machine checks and a separate model review. We evaluate test-then-train: a document is scored before its use as builder training data, paired with the seed on that same document. This operational localization is not verified causal attribution.

Our contribution is an editing implementation and a paired sequential diagnosis of transfer, instruction growth and failed extension proposals. Self-improvement here is supervised artifact revision, not learning without gold feedback. Additional task results and component ablations are pending.

\paragraph{Scope and shared infrastructure.} The companion procedural study \citep{anon2026orchestration} examines deterministic dispatch and assembly around fragment or closed decisions. We instead study instruction revision in a three-call whole-graph harness. The studies share UMR data, alignment and judge infrastructure, and our procedural comparisons reuse its runs; shared runs are not independent replication.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Annotating a complex ontology requires both an understanding of its structures and adherence to a given codebook. An annotator must recognize events, participants, and relations, then express them according to the project's definitions and conventions \citep{pustejovsky2012natural,kim2026refining}. This is especially difficult at the start of an annotation project, when examples are scarce. With enough annotated data, a parser can be trained; with little data, that route is difficult. Large language models (LLMs) offer a way to bootstrap: pretraining gives them a working understanding of linguistic structures and familiarity with structured output formats. Supplying the guideline can direct these capabilities toward the intended annotation scheme.

Prior work has paved this route. \citet{kim2026refining} show that reasoning models can apply annotation guidelines and that an iterative moderation process can improve the guidelines from annotation errors. Their experiments, however, concern named-entity recognition, a relatively simple, direct labeling task. Bootstrapping a complex ontology brings additional demands. Guidelines can be lengthy: the TimeML manual spans 71 pages \citep{sauri2006timeml}; annotation may also require external resources, such as PropBank frames for Uniform Meaning Representation (UMR) \citep{vangysel2021designing,palmer2005propbank}. Placing a long guideline in context does not ensure that a model follows it throughout: information in the middle is used poorly \citep{liu2024lost}, and agents lose details of long procedural instructions \citep{panavas2026handbook}.

We propose decomposing the guideline into a chain of steps and equipping those steps with tools, then improving this \emph{harness} through recursive self-improvement (RSI). We call this approach \sys (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}). A builder LLM derives a seed harness from the guideline; fine-grained feedback on a few annotated examples then guides revisions to step instructions, the workflow, and tools. The harness organizes the model's linguistic judgments and supports them with resource lookups and executable rules. It thus turns instructions and limited supervision into a working annotator, without fine-tuning.

Our contributions are threefold:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item A comprehensive guideline-and-harness optimization framework for bootstrapping complex annotation, with instructions, steps, and tools all open to refinement (\S\ref{sec:method}).
  \item Evidence that an annotator bootstrapped from instructions and a few examples can match or exceed state-of-the-art fine-tuned systems in matched evaluations (\S\ref{sec:main}).
  \item Evidence of continued evolution: harness improvements transfer to unseen texts and other annotators, while the builder creates and revises resource lookup tools and turns recurring errors into rules (\S\ref{sec:general}, \S\ref{sec:how}).
\end{enumerate}

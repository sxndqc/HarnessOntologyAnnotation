\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Annotating a complex ontology requires not only an understanding of its structures, but also codebooks and conventions given by a guideline. The latter part is the hardest at the start of an annotation project, when examples are scarce. Without enough annotated data, the route of finetuning is closed. Instead, large language models (LLMs) offer a way to bootstrap, whose pretraining gives them a working understanding of linguistic structures and familiarity with structured output formats. Supplying the guideline can direct these capabilities toward the intended annotation scheme.

Prior work has paved this route. \citet{kim2026refining} show that reasoning models can apply annotation guidelines and that an iterative moderation process can improve the guidelines from annotation errors, on named-entity recognition, a flat labeling task. Bootstrapping a complex ontology makes two further demands. a) Guidelines can be lengthy: the TimeML manual spans 71 pages \citep{sauri2006timeml} or even 300 pages for Uniform Meaning Representation (UMR) \citep{vangysel2021designing, bonn2024building}, and b) annotation may also require external resources, such as PropBank frames \citep{palmer2005propbank} for UMR. Models follow long guidelines unevenly known as ``lost-in-the-middle'' phenomenon \citep{liu2024lost}, and agents lose details of long procedural instructions \citep{panavas2026handbook}.

We propose \sys, decomposing the guideline into a chain of steps and equipping those steps with tools, then improving this \emph{harness} through recursive self-improvement (RSI) (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}). A builder LLM derives a seed harness from the guideline; fine-grained feedback on a few annotated examples then guides revisions to step instructions, the workflow, and tools. The harness organizes the model's linguistic judgments and supports them with resource lookups and executable rules.

Our contributions are threefold:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item We introduce a guideline-and-harness optimization framework for bootstrapping complex annotation, with editable instructions, steps, and tools (\S\ref{sec:method}).
  \item From a guideline and 50 annotated sentences, \sys surpasses some SOTA results, and harness refinement yields large gains across models (\S\ref{sec:main}, \S\ref{sec:controls}).
  % \item The learned harness generalizes to unseen texts and transfers unchanged to other annotators. Across rounds, the builder creates and revises resource lookup tools and turns recurring convention errors into executable rules (\S\ref{sec:general}, \S\ref{sec:how}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Data.}
\emph{UMR.} We use the test set of the first UMR parsing shared task \citep{stepanek2026first}, released with UMR~2.2 \citep{bonn2026uniform}. Its Chinese and English documents for which we have a cleaned gold annotation \gap{state the source and cleanup of this gold} (13 of the 15 Chinese and 4 of the 5 English test documents) are shuffled with a fixed seed and taken whole while the total stays at most 100 sentences: \nTestDocsZh{} Chinese documents (\nTestZh{} sentences) and the \nTestDocsEn{} English documents (\nTestEn{} sentences; two of them are the same text annotated twice, as in the shared task). Training groups are five consecutive sentences of one document, drawn from documents that share no sentence with the test set: Chinese UMR documents 0001--0018, and the fully annotated English documents of the release (most of it is partial AMR conversion without aspect, or Minecraft dialogue). \emph{TimeML.} TempEval-3 \citep{uzzaman2013semeval}: the test documents are \nTestDocsTml{} of the 20 platinum documents (\nTestTml{} sentences, chosen as above); training groups come from the 13 platinum documents that are not test documents, or, for a second trajectory, from TBAQ-cleaned (TimeBank~1.2 and AQUAINT, the TempEval-3 training data); all follow the TimeML 1.2.1 guidelines \citep{sauri2006timeml}. Each benchmark has ten training groups, used in a fixed random order, one per round; the builder never sees a test document.

\paragraph{Models.}
Annotators are Claude Haiku~5.5 (\texttt{claude-haiku-5-5}) and GPT-6.1-sol (\texttt{openai/gpt-6.1-sol} through OpenRouter); Claude Sonnet~5.5 writes the seed and is the builder in every round. Claude models run with adaptive thinking at the Messages API's default effort (Haiku medium, Sonnet high), GPT with medium reasoning effort. Each configuration was run once (answers are cached, so a repeated call is not a new sample); for UMR we repeated $H_0$ and $H_{10}$ with fresh samples to estimate run-to-run variation.

\paragraph{Conditions.}
\emph{Guideline prompt:} one call per sentence whose instructions are the whole guideline, followed by the sentence (no earlier sentences, no examples), with Haiku, Sonnet and GPT. \emph{Seed harness $H_0$:} the three-step harness written from the guideline, with each of the four generator/critic pairs of Haiku and GPT, and with Sonnet in both roles. \emph{\sys:} ten rounds of RSI with Haiku as generator and critic, testing every version $H_0,\ldots,H_{10}$; the final $H_{10}$ is also run, unchanged, with the other generator/critic pairs and with Sonnet in both roles. For TimeML we run two trajectories, with training groups from the platinum documents that are not test documents (the main one, from the same annotation project as the test set) and from TBAQ-cleaned. All conditions receive the same output-format instructions.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
UMR: AnCast++ \citep{sun2025ancastpp} sentence-graph F (\emph{F}, the main metric) and its comprehensive score with the document-level relations (\emph{Comp.}), after the cleanup of \citet{chun2024pipeline}, macro-averaged over documents, and the shared task's official \jumach{} score \citep{zeman2026word}, computed with \texttt{compare\_umr.pl} against the shared task's own test gold. \jumach{} maps nodes through the token alignment, so it also scores alignment, and its gold differs from the cleaned gold (Appendix~\ref{app:data}). TimeML: the official TempEval-3 scorer: temporal awareness TLINK F1 (\emph{TLINK}, the main metric), event and time-expression extent F1 and the value F1 of time expressions. The judges' per-check scores support the analysis.

\paragraph{Prior systems.}
We re-score earlier systems on exactly our test documents from their released outputs: the two UMR shared-task systems \citep{heinecke2026orange,devergnette2026sema}, fine-tuned on the shared task's training data, of which Orange is best on both Chinese and English; and the TempEval-3 end-to-end systems \citep{uzzaman2013semeval}, among them ClearTK-2 \citep{bethard2013cleartk}, the best participant, and TIPSem, the organizers' reference system, which is best on our documents.

\paragraph{Statistics.}
Differences between two conditions are paired over test documents (UMR: the per-document F; TimeML: the corpus-level TLINK F1 recomputed from per-document counts) with a bootstrap of 20{,}000 resamples; for UMR we also report a sentence-level bootstrap of per-sentence AnCast scores. With seven or four test documents and one run per configuration, the intervals describe the variation over documents, not over runs.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Benchmarks}
\label{sec:benchmarks}

\paragraph{UMR.} A UMR annotation of a sentence \citep{vangysel2021designing,bonn2024building} is a rooted graph in PENMAN notation whose nodes are concepts: events with PropBank-style senses \citep{palmer2005propbank} or language-specific lemmas, entities, named-entity types, and abstract rolesets such as \texttt{have-org-role-92}. Edges are participant roles (\texttt{:ARG0}, \ldots) and non-participant roles (\texttt{:temporal}, \texttt{:manner}, \ldots); attributes give aspect, polarity, mode, and the person and number of referring expressions. Each node is aligned to the words it comes from. A document-level graph per sentence adds temporal relations between events and times, modal relations between conceivers and events (who asserts what, with which strength), and coreference with earlier sentences. The guideline \citep{vangysel2021designing} has 365{,}742 characters. The resource files are the Chinese and English PropBank frame files, the UMR lists of abstract rolesets, relations and attribute values, and the guideline.

\paragraph{TimeML.} A TimeML annotation \citep{pustejovsky2003timeml}, following the TimeML 1.2.1 guidelines \citep{sauri2006timeml} as in TimeBank~1.2 \citep{pustejovsky2006timebank} and TempEval-3 \citep{uzzaman2013semeval}, marks time expressions (\texttt{TIMEX3}, with a type and a normalized value such as \texttt{2013-W11}), events (\texttt{EVENT}, with a class and the tense, aspect, polarity and modality of its instance), and temporal links (\texttt{TLINK}) of thirteen relation types between events, times and the document creation time, within and across sentences. The guideline has 115{,}146 characters and is the only resource file. The annotation of a sentence is the sentence with inline tags, followed by its TLINK lines.

\paragraph{Judges.} For UMR we use umrjudge: AnCast++ \citep{sun2024anchor,sun2025ancastpp} aligns predicted and gold nodes, and 25 checks in 11 groups compare the aligned graphs (nodes, senses, attributes, arguments, role labels, names, reentrancy, and the document-level temporal, modal and coreference relations; Appendix~\ref{app:judge}). For TimeML we built timemljudge on the same design from earlier evaluations: TempEval-3's extent and attribute measures for times and events \citep{uzzaman2013semeval}, and the TempEval-2 division of temporal links by what they connect \citep{verhagen2010semeval} (event--time in one sentence or across sentences, event--document time, event--event in one sentence or across, and time--time), each scored for which pairs are linked and for the relation of the pairs linked on both sides.

\subsection{Setup}

\paragraph{Data.}
\emph{UMR.} We use the test set of the first UMR parsing shared task \citep{stepanek2026first}, released with UMR~2.2 \citep{bonn2026uniform}. Its Chinese and English documents for which we have a cleaned gold annotation \gap{state the source and cleanup of this gold} (13 of the 15 Chinese and 4 of the 5 English test documents) are shuffled with a fixed seed and taken whole while the total stays at most 100 sentences: \nTestDocsZh{} Chinese documents (\nTestZh{} sentences) and the \nTestDocsEn{} English documents (\nTestEn{} sentences; two of them are the same text annotated twice, as in the shared task). Training groups are five consecutive sentences of one document, drawn from documents that share no sentence with the test set: Chinese UMR documents 0001--0018, and the three fully annotated English documents of the release (the rest are mostly partial AMR conversions without aspect, or Minecraft dialogue). \emph{TimeML.} The test documents are \nTestDocsTml{} of the 20 TempEval-3 platinum documents \citep{uzzaman2013semeval} (\nTestTml{} sentences, chosen as above). Training groups come from the 13 platinum documents that are not test documents or, for a second trajectory, from TBAQ-cleaned (TimeBank~1.2 and AQUAINT, the TempEval-3 training data). Each benchmark has ten training groups, used in a fixed random order, one per round; the builder never sees a test document.

\paragraph{Models.}
The annotators are Claude Haiku~5.5 (\texttt{claude-haiku-5-5}) and GPT-6.1-sol (\texttt{openai/gpt-6.1-sol} through OpenRouter); Claude Sonnet~5.5 writes the seed and is the builder in every round. Claude models run with adaptive thinking at the Messages API's default effort (Haiku medium, Sonnet high), GPT with medium reasoning effort. Each configuration was run once, with cached answers; for UMR we repeated $H_0$ and $H_{10}$ with fresh samples to measure run-to-run variation.

\paragraph{Conditions.}
\emph{Guideline prompt:} one call per sentence whose instructions are the whole guideline, followed by the sentence (no earlier sentences, no examples), with Haiku, Sonnet and GPT. \emph{Seed harness $H_0$:} the three-step harness written from the guideline, with each of the four generator/critic pairs of Haiku and GPT, and with Sonnet in both roles. \emph{\sys:} ten rounds of RSI with Haiku as generator and critic, testing every version $H_0,\ldots,H_{10}$; the final $H_{10}$ also runs unchanged with the other generator/critic pairs and with Sonnet in both roles. For TimeML, the main trajectory trains on the platinum documents that are not test documents, from the same annotation project as the test set, and a second one on TBAQ-cleaned. All conditions receive the same output-format instructions.

\paragraph{Controls.} Three controls separate what the harness adds from what a better guideline adds. The guideline \emph{through tools}: the instructions give only the guideline's address, and the model searches and reads the guideline (and, for UMR, the resource files) with tools; Haiku runs on all test documents and GPT on two documents per benchmark. A \emph{guideline-only round}: one round of RSI on the first training group that starts from the guideline prompt and may only edit or rewrite it (no steps, tools or rules, no length limit). \emph{Restricted RSI}: two rounds from the seed with the tool operations disabled, and with rule steps disabled. The GPT generator/critic pairs with $H_{10}$ run on the same two documents per benchmark. For TimeML we also test an explicit linking step (\S\ref{sec:link}).

\paragraph{Metrics.}
UMR: AnCast++ \citep{sun2025ancastpp} sentence-graph F (\emph{F}, the main metric) and its comprehensive score with the document-level relations (\emph{Comp.}), after the cleanup of \citet{chun2024pipeline}, macro-averaged over documents; and the shared task's official \jumach{} score \citep{zeman2026word}, computed with \texttt{compare\_umr.pl} against the shared task's own test gold. \jumach{} maps nodes through the token alignment, so it also scores alignment, and its gold differs from the cleaned gold (Appendix~\ref{app:data}). TimeML: the official TempEval-3 scorer, with temporal awareness TLINK F1 (\emph{TLINK}, the main metric), event and time-expression extent F1, and the value F1 of time expressions. The judges' per-check scores support the analysis.

\paragraph{Prior systems.}
We re-score earlier systems on exactly our test documents from their released outputs: the two UMR shared-task systems \citep{heinecke2026orange,devergnette2026sema}, fine-tuned on the shared task's training data, of which Orange is best on both Chinese and English; and the TempEval-3 end-to-end systems \citep{uzzaman2013semeval}, of which ClearTK-2 \citep{bethard2013cleartk} is best on temporal awareness and is the best earlier system we compare with. The organizers also report TIPSem \citep{llorens2010tipsem} as a reference that scores higher, and they separate it from the participants because some annotators used it to pre-label the platinum data \citep[\S2.2, \S5]{uzzaman2013semeval}; on our documents 73\% of the gold TLINK pairs are pairs that TIPSem links (ClearTK-2: 26\%). We report TIPSem as a reference, not as the state of the art.

\paragraph{Statistics.}
Differences between two conditions are paired over test documents (UMR: per-document F; TimeML: corpus-level TLINK F1 recomputed from per-document counts) with a bootstrap of 20{,}000 resamples; for UMR we also report a sentence-level bootstrap of per-sentence AnCast scores. The intervals describe variation over test documents; the repeated runs give the run-to-run variation.

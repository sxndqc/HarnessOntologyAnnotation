\subsection{Main results}
\label{sec:main}

Table~\ref{tab:main} gives every system on the same test documents.

\paragraph{The seed harness helps where the guideline is hard to apply} Written from the same guideline, the three-step seed improves over the Haiku prompt baseline on Chinese UMR (\mainSeedZh{} vs.\ \mainBaseHaikuZh{}) and on TimeML (TLINK \mainSeedTml{} vs.\ \mainBaseHaikuTml{}), ten points above the best earlier system (ClearTK-2, \sotaTlinkCleartk{}). On English UMR, where the prompt baseline is strong, the seed stays below it (\mainSeedEn{} vs.\ \mainBaseHaikuEn{}).

\paragraph{RSI improves the harness on held-out documents} After ten rounds of five sentences each, $H_{10}$ improves over the seed on both UMR test sets: Chinese F \mainSeedZh{}~$\rightarrow$~\mainLearnedZh{} (paired document bootstrap \gainLearnedZh{}, 95\% interval \gainLearnedZhLo{} to \gainLearnedZhHi{}) and English \mainSeedEn{}~$\rightarrow$~\mainLearnedEn{} (\gainLearnedEn{}, \gainLearnedEnLo{} to \gainLearnedEnHi{}). The comprehensive score rises more (\compSeedZh{}~$\rightarrow$~\compLearnedZh{}; \compSeedEn{}~$\rightarrow$~\compLearnedEn{}), and so does the official \jumach{} score (\jumachSeedZh{}~$\rightarrow$~\jumachLearnedZh{}; \jumachSeedEn{}~$\rightarrow$~\jumachLearnedEn{}). $H_{10}$ is above the best shared-task system on the same documents on every UMR metric (\jumach{} \sotaJumachZh{} and \sotaJumachEn{}; F \sotaFZh{} and \sotaFEn{}); that system was fine-tuned on the shared task's training data, and $H_{10}$ learned from 50 annotated sentences. Repeated runs of $H_0$ and $H_{10}$ with fresh samples differ by at most \repDiffLearnedZh{} F, well below the gains. On TimeML, temporal awareness stays level (\mainSeedTml{}~$\rightarrow$~\mainLearnedTml{}) while the linked pairs change (\S\ref{sec:how}).

\subsection{Guideline or harness?}
\label{sec:controls}

\input{tables/controls}

Table~\ref{tab:controls} compares, with Haiku in every role, ways of giving the model the guideline.

\paragraph{Reading the guideline through tools is worse than having it in the prompt} With only the guideline's address and tools to search and read it, Haiku falls below the prompt baseline everywhere, most on Chinese (F \ctlToolsZh{} vs.\ \ctlPromptZh{}). It looks up what it knows it needs, and the conventions it is missing go unread: on Chinese it writes English concepts (\texttt{define-01}) for Chinese words. A stronger reasoning model reads better: on two documents per benchmark, GPT-6.1-sol with tools reaches \subGptToolsZh{} (Chinese) and \subGptToolsEn{} (English) F, as high as $H_{10}$ on the same documents, while the harness keeps its lead on the Chinese document level and official score (\jumach{} \subLearnedHHJumachZh{} vs.\ \subGptToolsJumachZh{}; Appendix Table~\ref{tab:transfer}).

\paragraph{Editing the guideline fixes conventions, not structure} One round of reflection that may only edit the guideline appends 4--7K characters of ``house conventions'' to it. This fixes notation: \jumach{} rises from \ctlPromptJumachZh{} to \ctlGuideOneJumachZh{} on Chinese and from \ctlPromptJumachEn{} to \ctlGuideOneJumachEn{} on English, and on English, where the single pass is already strong, F reaches \ctlGuideOneEn{}, as high as $H_{10}$. On Chinese, F barely moves (\ctlPromptZh{}~$\rightarrow$~\ctlGuideOneZh{}) while the harness after the same round is at \ctlHarnessOneZh{}, and on TimeML the edited guideline (TLINK \ctlGuideOneTml{}) stays below the seed harness (\ctlSeedTml{}). A guideline edit states the data's notation; the harness also splits the work into draft, check and revision, and gives the model lookups.

\subsection{What RSI learns carries over}
\label{sec:general}

What a round learns can carry over to documents the builder never saw and to annotating models other than the one it watched; we call the second \emph{transfer}.

\paragraph{Across documents} Each round improves its own training group, by \trainGainZh{} (Chinese), \trainGainEn{} (English) and \trainGainTml{} (TimeML) points on average (Figure~\ref{fig:curves}, orange). For UMR these gains reach the test documents: the test score rises in most rounds (\roundsTestUpZh{} of 10 for Chinese, \roundsTestUpEn{} for English) and flattens after three to six groups, 15--30 annotated sentences, while later rounds increasingly fix what is particular to a group. Edits are kept without a held-out check, and three rounds per language lower the test score, by at most 1.8 points.

\paragraph{Across models (transfer)} $H_{10}$ was built with Haiku as generator and critic, so the builder saw only Haiku's errors. In a transfer run, $H_{10}$ is used unchanged and only the models that run its steps are replaced, and the run is compared with the seed $H_0$ run by the same models (rows marked ``transfer'' in Table~\ref{tab:main}). If RSI only patched Haiku's weaknesses, other models would not gain. They gain as much: GPT-6.1-sol in both roles rises from \mainSeedGGZh{} to \mainLearnedGGZh{} F on Chinese, Sonnet from \sonnetSeedFZh{} to \sonnetLearnedFZh{} (Chinese) and from \sonnetSeedFEn{} to \sonnetLearnedFEn{} (English), and on two documents per benchmark every Haiku/GPT pair gains 7 to 15 F on UMR (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:seed2x2}). At the seed, the four Haiku/GPT pairs are within three points of each other on every benchmark, while RSI adds 6 to 11: the harness matters more than the choice of annotating model. What the builder wrote is about the scheme, not about the model it watched.

\label{sec:how}

\paragraph{Conventions first, then lookups} Broken down by judge check (Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:judge}), the largest UMR gains are on conventions that the guideline states differently from the data or not at all: document-level modal relations (Chinese 0~$\rightarrow$~50, English 0~$\rightarrow$~32), attribute names and values, entity concepts. Next come PropBank senses (88~$\rightarrow$~94, 67~$\rightarrow$~80) and core arguments (52~$\rightarrow$~63, 42~$\rightarrow$~55); aspect, coreference and document-level temporal relations change little. In the ablation (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}), the learned instructions carry most of the gain (with the seed's instructions, F falls by \ablSeedSkillsZhDelta{} and \ablSeedSkillsEnDelta{}); removing the tools hardly changes F but costs the checks the tools serve, senses (94~$\rightarrow$~78, 80~$\rightarrow$~73) and core arguments; removing the rule steps changes little. Two rounds of RSI without tools or without rule steps agree (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:shortabl}).

\paragraph{The builders invent tools and rules} Told only that PropBank files exist, each UMR builder wrote a lookup in the first rounds: \texttt{lookup\_frame} over the Chinese frame files (round 2), which also answers ``no frame'' with the UMR convention for such predicates, and \texttt{roleset\_lookup} for English (round 1), replaced in round 9 by a version that reads sense names from the XML frames after the builder noticed wrong sense choices. With $H_{10}$, \toolUseZh\% (Chinese) and \toolUseEn\% (English) of Haiku's calls use the tool. Rule steps were added more often (\stepsAppliedZh{} and \stepsAppliedEn{}), turning recurring convention errors into code; no new model step was proposed (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:ops}). When a skill approached the 10{,}000-character limit, the builder consolidated it, and the final harnesses have \sizeLastZh{}K, \sizeLastEn{}K and \sizeLastTml{}K characters of instructions, against 366K and 115K for the guidelines.

\paragraph{TimeML: which pairs to link}\label{sec:link} The judge shows what temporal awareness hides: from $H_0$ to $H_{10}$ the harness links the right pairs more often (event--document time 34~$\rightarrow$~45, event--event within a sentence 43~$\rightarrow$~55 pair F), but labels these pairs less accurately (75~$\rightarrow$~66). The seed links events to the document time that the gold leaves unlinked (123 of its 162 links) and finds few within-sentence event pairs (63 of 174). A hand-written link step that rebuilds each sentence's links as a sparse backbone, with or without a rule linking time expressions to the document time, lowers temporal awareness (Table~\ref{tab:link}). Given to RSI, the step becomes something the builder can tune: in one round it rewrote the step after the gold's practice (dependents of a clause to its main event, the main event to both its time expression and the document time) and reached \lnkLinkRsiOne{}, the best TimeML harness in our experiments and 13 points above ClearTK-2; the second round reduced over-linking across sentences and lost part of the gain (\lnkLinkRsiTwo{}).

\paragraph{The builder learns the gold it sees} Trained on TimeBank and AQUAINT, whose links are much sparser than the platinum gold, the harness learns to link less: recall falls (\tlinkrSeedTml{}~$\rightarrow$~\altTlinkRLearnedTml{}) and temporal awareness drops to \altTlinkLearnedTml{} (grey in Figure~\ref{fig:curves}). The loop adapts the annotator to the gold it is given and cannot tell a project's convention from an accident of a corpus. The builders' own suggestions point the same way: 65 of the 75 suggestions of the three main trajectories concern the gold, 34 of them inconsistencies a human moderator should resolve.

\paragraph{Case study} The English trajectory shows the order of the work (Appendix~\ref{app:case}). Round~1 replaced the guideline's notation with the data's in all three skills and added the PropBank tool; rounds~2--6 worked on the document level (modal relations, coreference direction, clean-up rules); rounds~7--10 turned to semantics (aspect cues, phrasal verbs, sense distinctions). In several rounds the builder found that \skill{critique} had ``fixed'' correct annotations and restricted its checklist, in line with the finding that self-correction without an external signal often hurts \citep{huang2024large,kamoi2024when}.

\begin{abstract}
Annotating text with a complex ontology takes both an understanding of the structures involved and close adherence to a long guideline, and at the start of a project there is too little annotated data to train a parser. We present \sys, which bootstraps an annotator from the guideline and a few annotated sentences without fine-tuning. A builder LLM writes a seed harness of model steps from the guideline alone, then improves it by recursive self-improvement on groups of five gold sentences: it summarizes the errors a fine-grained judge reports, reflects on their causes, and edits the instructions, adds rule steps and writes tools over resource files such as PropBank, subject only to machine checks. On the Chinese and English test documents of the UMR parsing shared task, ten rounds (50 annotated sentences) raise a harness run by Claude Haiku from \mainSeedZh{} to \mainLearnedZh{} (Chinese) and from \mainSeedEn{} to \mainLearnedEn{} (English) AnCast++ F, and to \jumachLearnedZh{} and \jumachLearnedEn{} on the official \jumach{} score, above the best fine-tuned shared-task system on the same documents (\sotaJumachZh{}, \sotaJumachEn{}). The builders write PropBank lookup tools when told only that frame files exist, and the learned harness transfers unchanged to other annotators (Sonnet, GPT-6.1-sol), while the choice of generator and critic models matters far less than the harness. Editing the guideline alone fixes notation but, on Chinese, not the graphs, and reading the guideline through tools helps only a strong reasoning model. On TimeML the harness learns which events and times to link but not how they are related, and it adopts the link density of whatever gold it is trained on; an explicit linking step, useless as written by hand, becomes the best TimeML harness after one round of RSI. A guideline plus a handful of examples suffices to learn the conventions of a complex scheme and the tools it needs; judgments that no resource supports remain the model's.
\end{abstract}

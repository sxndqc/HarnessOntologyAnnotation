\begin{abstract}
Complex annotation scheme requires structural understanding and adherence to detailed guidelines, yet new projects often lack enough data to train a parser. We present \sys, which bootstraps an annotator from a guideline and a few examples without fine-tuning. A builder LLM turns the guideline into annotation steps and uses recursive self-improvement to refine instructions, tools, and the workflow from fine-grained error feedback. With just a few dozen annotated sentences, \sys improves Chinese and English UMR AnCast++ F by \gainLearnedZh{} and \gainLearnedEn{}, respectively, and exceeds the best fine-tuned shared-task system on the official score for the same test documents. On TimeML, refining an explicit linking step reaches \lnkLinkRsiOne{} temporal-awareness F1, above the best TempEval-3 participant under matched evaluation. We further prove that the learned harness transfers unchanged across annotating models.
\end{abstract}

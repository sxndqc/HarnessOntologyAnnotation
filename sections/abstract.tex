\begin{abstract}
Annotating text with a complex ontology requires both an understanding of its structures and close adherence to a long guideline, and a new annotation project has too little data to train a parser. We present \sys, which bootstraps an annotator from a guideline and a few annotated sentences without fine-tuning. A builder LLM writes a seed harness of model steps from the guideline alone and improves it by recursive self-improvement: on each group of five gold sentences, it reads the errors reported by a fine-grained judge, identifies their causes, and edits step instructions, adds rule steps, or writes tools over resource files, subject to machine checks. On the Chinese and English test documents of the UMR parsing shared task, ten rounds (50 annotated sentences) raise a harness run by Claude Haiku from \mainSeedZh{} to \mainLearnedZh{} (Chinese) and from \mainSeedEn{} to \mainLearnedEn{} (English) AnCast++ F, and reach \jumachLearnedZh{} and \jumachLearnedEn{} on the official \jumach{} score, above the best fine-tuned shared-task system on the same documents (\sotaJumachZh{}, \sotaJumachEn{}). The builder writes PropBank lookup tools when told only that frame files exist, and the learned harness transfers unchanged to Sonnet and GPT-6.1-sol; the choice of generator and critic models matters far less than the harness. Editing the guideline alone corrects notation, while the step decomposition improves the Chinese graphs. On TimeML, the harness learns which events and times to link and adapts to the link density of its training gold; with an explicit linking step, one round of RSI reaches \lnkLinkRsiOne{} temporal-awareness F1, 13 points above the best TempEval-3 participant. A guideline and a handful of examples suffice to learn the conventions of a complex scheme and the tools it needs.
\end{abstract}

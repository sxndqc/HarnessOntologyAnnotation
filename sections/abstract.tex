\begin{abstract}
Annotating text with a rich ontology, such as UMR graphs, TimeML or a policy codebook, is still done by trained people following long guidelines. LLMs follow instructions, but a scheme's conventions are external knowledge, and the harness that turns an LLM into an annotator (per-step instructions, tools, the chain of calls) is built by hand through error analysis. We ask whether an LLM can build that harness itself from a few gold documents. \sys traces every step of a harness, attributes each divergence from gold to the responsible step and scheme category, applies typed edits to that step only, and admits new tools and steps only through tests and a reviewer, with a guard against copying gold. We evaluate test-then-train and pair each score with the seed harness on the same unseen document. On Chinese UMR, the learned harness gains \gainF{} sentence F on unseen documents (95\% CI \gainFLo--\gainFHi; \gainFPos{} of \nPaired{} documents) \gap{and beats in-context learning and guideline-only refinement with the same documents}. The gain is scheme convention written into the generator's instructions, not better semantic judgment; a verification step adds \critFirst{} F, and no proposed tool is admitted. The loop almost never deletes, the harness grows \sizeFactorInt-fold while the gain stops growing, and a 9B builder makes the annotator worse. \gap{TimeML and manifesto coding.}
\end{abstract}
